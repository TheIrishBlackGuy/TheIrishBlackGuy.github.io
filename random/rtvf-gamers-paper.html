This article is presenting the argument that the term ‘gamer’ can be classified as an identity descriptor similar to a person's ethnicity or race and in the same way that one would apply a gender or sexuality to themselves. It also addresses that there is no necessary rank or priority among these descriptors. Because of this base of the article, it was thought that game companies direct their games towards those that do identify as gamers. Part of this issue, as of the writing of the article, is that the dominant group of people associated with the term are heterosexual, white males. Due to them being the target, some of the more popular genres of the time were first-person-shooters, sports games, and/or ‘guy-in-situation’ games.

A decade has passed since the article was written and times have changed. There are many varying types of gamers to the point where people now specify which one they align with. Via mediums of streaming, posting videos online, and other means of showcasing, gamers do not hide themselves as they once did. Now in the gaming community, the previous lack of under-represented people is not one of gaming’s biggest concerns. Because of this now, there are a wide variety of games featuring types of people across multiple backgrounds, ethnicities, races, and genders. The previously mentioned genres are still available however, they do not make up the majority as they once did. There are many more niche games that may not rank high in some charts, they still garner a wide recognition as they are brought to the forefront by online creators.

A popular game in recent years and one praised for its inclusion would be Horizon Forbidden West. In the game, you play as Aloy, a female character that is later confirmed to be queer. She roams a post-post-apocalyptic world that has been reclaimed by nature and the robotic creatures of the game's plot. The inhabitants of the world exist in villages and tribes scattered across the world. These people are not just in the game to act as background characters. There are over 100 named characters and each has their own story and personality. Many characters are part of a wide variety of people in terms of ethnicities, sexuality and genders.
